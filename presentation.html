Problem statement

Eavesdropping

An attacker may read packet contents sent over an untrusted network.

Modification

An attacker may alter data without immediately knowing the original content.

Replay

A captured valid packet may be resent to recreate an earlier action.

Unauthorized access

Invalid packets must not reveal protected information.

Objectives

  • Demonstrate real AES-256-GCM encryption and authentication.
  • Construct an inspectable representation of an ESP packet.
  • Detect ciphertext tampering and reject invalid packets.
  • Track sequence numbers and reject replayed packets.
  • Present live packet events in a professional dashboard.

ESP packet flow

1. Protect

Generate nonce and encrypt plaintext with AES-GCM.

2. Transmit

Send SPI, sequence number, nonce, ciphertext and tag.

3. Receive

Cryptographic design

AES-256-GCM

AES uses a 256-bit key. GCM provides encryption plus authentication.

Nonce

A unique random 96-bit nonce is generated for every packet.

Associated data

SPI | Packet ID | Sequence Number

Changing ciphertext or ESP associated data invalidates authentication.

Attack simulations

Ciphertext tampering

One byte is flipped. GCM authentication fails and plaintext is not revealed.

Replay attack

The previously received sequence number is detected and the packet is rejected.

Conclusion

SecureTunnel demonstrates how IPsec ESP concepts protect communication through confidentiality, integrity, authentication and replay resistance.

Scope clarity

This is an educational ESP simulator using real AES-GCM cryptography. It does not implement IKEv2 or a full operating-system network tunnel.
